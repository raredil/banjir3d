# Fast fine cells (dual grid, building porosity): sources and facts (2026-11-08)

- Sanders, B.F., Schubert, J.E., Gallegos, H.A. (2008) Integral formulation of shallow-water equations with anisotropic porosity for urban flood modeling. J. Hydrology 362, 19–38. doi:10.1016/j.jhydrol.2008.08.009. Fact used: volumetric porosity (storage) per cell, areal porosity (conveyance) per cell edge, directional. Later work: edge void fraction is sensitive to mesh layout; Sanders et al. recommend gap connectivity. (verified: search 2026-11-08, several catalogue/repository pages)
- Kim, B., Sanders, B.F., Famiglietti, J.S., Guinot, V. (2015) J. Hydrology 523, 680–692: anisotropic porosity well suited to whole-city prediction, misses point-scale features (wakes, reflections). (from search excerpt; not cited in the app)
- Sanders, B.F., Schubert, J.E. (2019) PRIMo: Parallel raster inundation model. Adv. Water Resources 126, 79–95. doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2019.02.007. Fact used: solves the shallow-water equations on an upscaled grid far coarser than the raster DTM with a subgrid approach (dual grid), results at fine resolution; used for 3 m flood mapping of ~7,000 km² (Los Angeles). Author order differs between catalogues (Sanders first in most). (verified: search 2026-11-08)
- Schubert and Sanders (2012), already in references: building-block vs porosity treatments; porosity much faster at similar skill.
- Measured here (verification/fine-speed/results.json): CPU solver 11–18 million cell updates/s (Node 22, one core); 2 m blocks vs porosity 6/10 m on a 250 m Johar Baru box: 37x / 177x faster, mean depth −2 cm, RMSE 0.20–0.25 m, CSI 0.64–0.72.
- Conveyance rule tested: harmonic mean of column open shares vs straight open lanes: CSI 0.72/0.64 (harmonic, 6/10 m) vs 0.71/0.66 (lanes); no clear winner, harmonic kept (cheaper, no mesh-alignment bias).
